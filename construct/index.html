yaronlab.tools/construct

Plasmid Construct Builder

Assemble a synthesis-ready coding sequence: terminal fusions and affinity tags in the right order, linkers, codon optimization for mammalian or E. coli expression, Kozak or Shine-Dalgarno elements, and restriction-site awareness for the cloning step.

1Expression host and codon strategy

Codon weights are derived from coding sequences of highly expressed genes in each host (see the Method note at the bottom of the page).

to %
Weighted sampling draws codons in proportion to their usage in the reference set, which avoids long tracts of a single codon while keeping rare codons out. The seed makes a run reproducible, so you can quote it in a methods section.

4Translation signals and flanks

Guard bases are added outside each flanking restriction site so the enzyme can cut a linear fragment efficiently. Selected flank sites are automatically excluded from the internal avoidance list.

5Restriction sites to keep out of the insert

Selected sites are removed from the coding sequence by synonymous codon changes, so they stay unique for cloning. The full construct is also scanned and reported.

Sequence and codon-usage sources

  • Solubility and affinity partner sequences: UniProt (MBP P0AEX9, GST P08515, SUMO Q12306 and P55854, thioredoxin P0AA25, NusA P0AFF6).
  • HaloTag and SNAP-tag sequences: NCBI Protein synthetic-construct records, verified against the canonical termini and active-site residues.
  • Codon-usage tables: computed from coding sequences of highly expressed genes retrieved from NCBI (E. coli K-12 MG1655, 97 sequences; human RefSeq Select, 49 transcripts).

Method note. Codon usage weights were computed from the coding sequences of highly expressed genes retrieved from NCBI: 97 E. coli K-12 MG1655 sequences (ribosomal proteins, elongation factors, glycolytic enzymes, abundant outer-membrane and chaperone genes; 26,556 codons) and 49 human RefSeq Select transcripts (cytoskeletal, ribosomal, glycolytic and chaperone genes; 19,131 codons). For each amino acid the relative adaptiveness w of a codon is its frequency divided by that of the most frequent synonymous codon, which is the standard basis for the codon adaptation index reported in the quality checks.

Fusion partner sequences. The 20 fluorescent proteins are the canonical records from FPbase; the solubility and affinity partners (MBP, GST, SUMO, thioredoxin, NusA) are from UniProt, with MBP given as the mature chain and both SUMO variants trimmed to the C-terminal Gly-Gly so SUMO or SENP proteases leave a native N-terminus. Accessions are listed in each element's note. Protein elements are codon-optimized for the chosen host exactly like the gene of interest.

Check every construct against your own plasmid map before ordering, and follow your institution's biosafety review for any sequence you send for synthesis.
